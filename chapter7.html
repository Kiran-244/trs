CHAPTER VII

Questions, Stories,
and Lessons Beyond the Syllabus

The conversations that quietly became the lessons I remembered most.

Scroll to Begin

2023 – 2024

Looking back now, I don't think the most memorable part of your classes was the syllabus.

It was everything that existed around it.

The stories.

The questions.

The conversations.

Somehow, an English lecture would quietly become a lesson about people, perspective, or life itself.

And those were often the moments that stayed with me the longest.

One afternoon, only a handful of us had reached the classroom.

It was just me and two of my classmates sitting on the front bench, as we almost always did.

The rest of the class hadn't arrived yet.

Instead of beginning the lecture, you smiled and started talking to the three of us.

You told us a story from your childhood.

I may not remember every detail exactly the way it happened.

Memory has a way of changing little things over time.

But I remember how it made me feel.

You told us how your father had decided to change your school.

You and two of your friends had gone to a new school together, hoping it would be a fresh beginning.

One little detail has stayed with me all these years.

You told us that one of your friends stumbled and fell while walking.

She had carefully styled her hair in a puff that morning, and the students around her burst into laughter.

It was such a simple story.

The kind of memory most people would probably forget.

Yet somehow, I never did.

Eventually, things didn't work out there, and all of you returned to your old school.

There were playful remarks.

Curious questions.

The kind of teasing children often don't think much about, even though it sometimes stays with us for years.

Looking back, I don't think I remember that story because it was extraordinary.

I remember it because you chose to share it with us.

Moments like that reminded me that teachers don't simply appear at the front of a classroom.

They, too, were once students.

They had childhood memories, awkward moments, friendships, and experiences that quietly shaped the people they would become.

I think that's one of the reasons your classes always felt so genuine.

You never hesitated to share those little pieces of your life with us, and in doing so, you made learning feel personal.

Looking back now, I realize those conversations taught us far more than the lesson planned for that day.

You never discouraged curiosity.

You welcomed it.

You created a classroom where different opinions could exist together, where students felt comfortable speaking their minds, and where a question often mattered just as much as an answer.

"Years fade. Chapters end. Textbooks change. But the conversations that awaken our minds have a way of staying with us forever."

Some lessons are taught from books.

The ones I remember most began with,

"Let me tell you a story..."
