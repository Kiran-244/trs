The Lessons That Never Appeared in a Textbook

Semester II • 2024

There were many things I admired about you.

Your communication skills.

Your humility.

Your kindness.

But more than anything...

It was the way you treated people.

I never once saw you raise your voice just to make students listen.

Respect came naturally.

You never believed fear was the foundation of discipline.

You believed in creating comfort.

And because of that...

Students genuinely wanted to participate.

One particular incident has stayed with me ever since.

Some students from the Mathematics department felt that your pace of teaching was a little too fast.

Eventually, the concern reached the Head of the Mathematics Department.

You walked in with the same calm smile as always.

Then, with a wonderfully sarcastic smile, you began...

"My dear students..."

I still remember those words vividly.

There was a playful sarcasm in your voice—not the kind that hurts, but the kind that instantly captures everyone's attention.

You spoke about how some students were finding it difficult to keep up with the pace of the lectures.

You assured everyone that you would slow down whenever necessary.

But what impressed me wasn't your explanation.

It was your character.

You never asked who had complained.

You never defended yourself.

You never made anyone feel guilty for speaking honestly.

You protected the student's dignity without anyone even realizing it.

Looking back now, I think that required far more wisdom than simply teaching grammar or literature.

It showed me that true confidence isn't offended by feedback.

It listens.

It reflects.

And when necessary...

It adapts.

"The best teachers don't just educate minds.
They protect hearts."

That day, you didn't just teach another English lecture.

You quietly demonstrated
what grace,
humility,
and leadership truly look like.
