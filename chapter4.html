CHAPTER IV

The Day I
Discovered
Something
About Myself

February 2024

One day, I came across an announcement from the English Department.

They were organizing a Poetry Writing Competition.

It sounded interesting.

My friends had already decided to participate.

I had always enjoyed English.

Throughout school, I happily took part in storytelling competitions, poetry recitations, speeches, and anything that gave me a chance to stand on a stage.

But writing poetry...

That was different.

For some reason, I had always believed poetry was for people who had endless time to think, write, and pour their emotions onto paper.

It never felt like something I could do.

Then came the Poetry Competition.

February 2, 2024.

Strange how some dates stay with us forever.

My friends were participating.

So I found myself wondering,

"Maybe I should try too."

The evening before the competition, I started to make the muscle of writing with my roommate.

She listened patiently and gave me honest feedback.

The next morning, I sat down with a rough sheet of paper.

I wrote.

Crossed out lines.

Changed words.

Read it again.

When I was finally satisfied, I carefully copied it onto the final sheet that would be submitted.

Before handing it in, I quietly folded the rough draft and slipped it back into my bag.

I couldn't explain why.

I just wanted to keep it.

It was the first poem I had ever written.

Somehow, I knew it mattered.

I wasn't surprised because I had won.

I was surprised because I had almost never tried.

Looking back, I don't think that competition changed my life because of the certificate.

It changed my mindset.

Until then, I had convinced myself that writing poetry wasn't meant for someone like me.

Your classroom had quietly encouraged me to think differently—to express, to question, and finally, to create.

For a few moments, I simply stood there in disbelief.

It wasn't just because I had won.

It was because I had almost never participated at all.

Until then, I had convinced myself that writing poetry wasn't meant for someone like me.

That single competition quietly changed the way I looked at every new opportunity.

I realized something that has stayed with me ever since.

"You may never know what you're capable of
unless you first give yourself the chance to try."

Every day, we lose countless battles—not because we fail, but because we never step onto the battlefield.

Perhaps I could have been a swimmer.

Perhaps I could have been a martial artist.

Perhaps I could have been many things I never even imagined.

But I'll never know unless I dare to begin.

Looking back now, I don't think the greatest reward was the certificate.

It was discovering that the limits I believed in were often the ones I had created myself.

I still have that rough sheet.

Not because it won a competition...
but because it reminds me
that every journey begins
with the courage to simply try.

I still have that rough sheet.

Not because it won a competition...
but because it reminds me of the day
I stopped deciding what I couldn't do
before I had even tried.
