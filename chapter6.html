Semester II • 2024

Do you remember...

Once, during one of our English lectures, you asked all of us,

"Who do you think God is?"

We had such an interesting discussion that day.

Everyone had a different perspective.

Some spoke about religion.

Some spoke about belief.

Some simply shared what God meant to them.

When it was my turn, I answered,

"For me, God is hope. God is faith."

Looking back now, I think I know exactly why I gave that answer.

We had just received our first-semester results that very day.

I was genuinely happy with the SGPA I had earned.

There was a quiet sense of gratitude within me.

Perhaps that gratitude became my definition of God that day.

Then you shared something deeply personal with us.

You told us that one of your cousins had recently passed away.

You spoke about how devastated the family was...

How his mother and sister were crying inconsolably.

And then you said something that has stayed with me ever since.

In moments like those...

What can we really say?

We can't tell them to have hope.

We cannot ask them to keep faith in god

Because sometimes, those words are not capable of giving someone the strength to carry on.

I remember thinking about that discussion long after the lecture had ended.

I usually love these kinds of existential questions.

Partly because I've always been fascinated by philosophy.

But also because there is never just one correct answer.

Every answer reflects a person's experiences...

Their beliefs.

Their perspective.

And perhaps, a little piece of who they are.

That was something I always admired about your classes.

They were never confined to the syllabus.

A simple question could become an unforgettable conversation.

I remember this happening during our debate competitions as well.

You always brought thoughtful ideas into the classroom.

Ideas that challenged us to think instead of simply agree.

You never wanted us to memorize opinions.

You wanted us to form our own.

Another thing I admired was your easy-going nature.

You never expected us to fill pages and pages of notebooks.

Of course, we'd jot down the important points whenever you dictated them.

But your classes never felt like a race to complete notes.

They felt like conversations.

There was always room to ask questions.

To disagree respectfully.

To see an idea from another perspective.

Looking back, I don't remember every grammar rule we studied.

But I still remember that discussion about God.

"Some conversations end when the bell rings.
The best ones continue quietly in our minds for years."

Thank you, Ma'am,

for teaching us
not just English,
but how to think,
question,
and understand life.
