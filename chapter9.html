CHAPTER IX

The Student Within the Teacher

The people who teach us are often still learning themselves.

Scroll to Begin

Semester II • 2024

There are many teachers I have learned from.

Many classrooms I have sat in.

Many lectures that began with attendance and ended with the ringing of a bell.

With time, most of them quietly became memories.

But every once in a while, you meet someone who stays.

Not because they demanded to be remembered.

Not because they tried to be extraordinary.

But because of the countless little things they did without ever realizing someone was paying attention.

A kind word.

A thoughtful question.

A story shared before the lecture began.

A smile that made an ordinary morning feel lighter.

Looking back now, I don't think there was one defining moment that made you unforgettable.

It was everything together.

Every lecture.

Every conversation.

Every story.

Every quiet lesson hidden between the lines.

Somewhere along the way, without even realizing it, you became one of the people I knew I would remember long after university.

When I first walked into your classroom, I simply hoped to improve my English.

I never imagined I would leave with something much more valuable.

You reminded me that confidence can be quiet.

That kindness and authority can exist together.

That curiosity deserves a place in every classroom.

And that some of the most meaningful lessons are the ones that never appear in a textbook.

Sometimes I wonder whether teachers ever truly know the difference they make.

Whether they realize that years later, a student can still remember a story they told...

A question they asked...

Or a simple conversation before the rest of the class arrived.

Perhaps they don't.

Maybe that's what makes teaching such a beautiful profession.

The seeds are planted quietly.

Long before anyone sees what they grow into.
