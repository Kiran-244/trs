One Last Thing

Guru Purnima • 2026

Every story eventually reaches its final page.

This one does too.

But before I close this book, there is one last thing I want to say.

Looking back now, I realize this story was never really about English.

It was about a teacher who quietly found a permanent place in the memories of one of her students.

If someone were to ask me years from now about my university life, I don't think I would begin by talking about grades, assignments, or examinations.

I would begin with the people.

Because when time passes, that is what we remember.

And somewhere among those memories...

There will always be an English teacher named Ms. Binderpal Kaur.

Maybe that's why I wanted to write this little book.

So that, even if only for a few minutes, you could see yourself through the eyes of one of your students.

So, here is my little confession, tucked between these pages rather than spoken aloud: I think the world is a little lovelier for having you in it, and my own little corner of it certainly is.

I don't know if there is a perfect word for this,I suppose I could call it gratitude, admiration, or fondness, and all of those would be true. But somehow, none of them feels quite enough on its own, it's the kind that asks for nothing and doesn't need to become anything more than what it is. Perhaps there isn't.

Perhaps it is enough to let it exist, quietly and sincerely, without trying too hard to define what it is that i feel about you.

Sometimes, I wonder whether teachers ever realize how deeply they influence their students. Maybe they don't. Maybe that's why I wanted to create this little diary. So that, just once, you could see yourself through the eyes of one of your students. Not as an English teacher. But as someone who quietly inspired confidence, curiosity, and a love for learning.
